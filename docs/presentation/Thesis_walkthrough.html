Motivation

Same label, possibly different construct

Benchmarks are the main instrument for comparing LLM behaviour, yet they are better understood as artifacts of the institutions that build them than as neutral measures (Raji et al., 2021; Branford & Kraft, 2026). Bias benchmarks are built by industry, academia and affected communities, each with its own idea of what a social bias is (Blodgett et al., 2021).

BBQ, which commercial model evaluations still report, defines nine U.S.-centric dimensions. Community-built benchmarks such as HESEIA and WinoQueer stay close to lived experience but rarely enter industry evaluation loops. The thesis asks whether benchmarks that use the same category names actually measure the same thing.

Research question

Do institutional and community-built bias benchmarks operationalize the same demographic and intersecting constructs at the level of dataset content?

RQ1Internal coherence: does each benchmark's own category form a coherent cluster of content?
RQ2Convergence: do same-labelled categories from different benchmarks land in the same semantic region?
RQ3Origin: does the community-built benchmark cover territory that institutional ones leave out?
?Open question
  • BBQ was built by academics but is adopted by industry. Is "institutional vs. community" the right contrast, or should origin be "where it is used" rather than "who built it"?
Relevant literature

Three strands meet in this thesis

Bias measurement is contestedDefinitions of "bias" are vague and inconsistent across NLP papers (Blodgett et al., 2020), benchmarks carry construction pitfalls (Blodgett et al., 2021), and BBQ shows selection and omitted-variable bias (Powers et al., 2024).
Benchmark content divergesSame-named classes are incompatible across hate-speech datasets (Fortuna et al., 2020). StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs cover different facets of gender stereotypes (Zakizadeh & Pilehvar, 2025). Construction choices alone shift bias scores (Selvam et al., 2023). QA benchmarks under-represent demographics and regions (Kraft et al., 2025).
Clustering as measurementSOFA clusters stereotype content with embeddings, UMAP and HDBSCAN (Marchiori Manerba et al., 2024). Text-as-data work treats such output as a measure that needs validation (Grimmer & Stewart, 2013; Ying et al., 2022; Nelson, 2020).

Gap: to my knowledge, no work compares the semantic structure of bias-benchmark content against the benchmarks' stated categories, across institutional and community-built benchmarks and across languages.

Construct

Content validity of social-bias categories

Each benchmark category (gender, race, religion, …) is a systematized version of a contested background concept. Following Jacobs & Wallach (2021) and Wallach et al. (2025), the thesis examines content validity: whether an operationalization captures the substantive nature of the construct it claims to measure.

Measurement instrument

Two instruments: the benchmark, and my pipeline that audits it

Wallach et al. (2024), following Adcock & Collier (2001), separate four levels. A bias benchmark is the instrument at level three, and the thesis examines that level's content. Because the clustering pipeline produces the evidence about the benchmarks, it is itself an instrument and has to be validated in its own right.

Theoretical approach

Intersectional and decolonial feminist lens

Demographic axes are treated as socially constructed classifications, not fixed variables. Gender and sexuality are read through decolonial feminism, as categories imposed through colonization (Lugones, 2010). Intersectionality means axes are co-constituted, not additive (Crenshaw, 1989). Data feminism asks to rethink binaries and hierarchies in data (Klein & D'Ignazio, 2024).

Empirically, this becomes one testable contrast: do a benchmark's intersectional items form their own territory (co-constitutive), or fall inside one parent axis (additive)?

?Open questions
  • Co-occurring labels (HESEIA) or two-axis categories (BBQ's Race×Gender) are a thin operationalization of intersectionality. Is there a better computational handle?
  • How many axes to analyse: all eleven, or the four that every benchmark shares (age, gender, disability, race)?
Methodology and justification

Each step has a precedent and a check

The overall design follows computational grounded theory (Nelson, 2020): clusters detect patterns, close reading interprets them, and classifiers plus sensitivity checks confirm them.

Pipeline

Two branches share one frozen embedding space

Branch A clusters items without labels. Branch B trains one probe per identity dimension on HESEIA, the community reference frame. They meet when the probes are projected onto the clusters: which dimension, from the community's standpoint, drives each BBQ cluster?

With BERTopic's defaults (English all-MiniLM-L6-v2), 610 items (4.3%) were noise; raising min_topic_size to 25 gave 31 topics. The topic count sits near BBQ's template count, and keywords such as "card, pulled, snap, amex" describe one SES scenario about paying with a SNAP card. If clusters are templates, RQ1 looks perfectly coherent for a trivial reason. Notebook 01 therefore changes the encoder, the text unit and the template control, and scores clusters against template IDs to test this.

?Open questions
